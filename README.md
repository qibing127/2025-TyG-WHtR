# Association Between TyG-WHtR (Triglyceride Glucose-Waist-to-Height Ratio) and Colorectal Cancer: A Retrospective Analysis Based on NHANES Cross-Sectional Data

## Abstract
### Objective: 
This study aimed to comprehensively investigate the association between the Triglyceride Glucose-Waist-to-Height Ratio (TyG-WHtR) and colorectal cancer (CRC), assessing whether TyG-WHtR could serve as a cost-effective preliminary screening indicator for identifying high-risk populations, particularly in resource-limited areas.
### Methods: 
We analyzed data from ten consecutive cycles (1999–2018) of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). Participants without colorectal cancer (CRC) diagnostic information, missing TyG-WHtR data, or essential baseline data were excluded, leaving 3,778 individuals (224 with CRC, 3,554 controls). Propensity score matching (PSM) balanced educational level and the family poverty income ratio (PIR), and inverse probability treatment weighting (IPTW) was employed to preserve maximum sample size. Weighted logistic regression models assessed associations between TyG-WHtR (continuous and quartiles) and CRC, providing odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Additional subgroup analyses, restricted cubic spline (RCS) models, XGBoost-based machine-learning, and mediation analyses of laboratory markers were conducted.
### Results: 
After PSM, TyG-WHtR was significantly elevated in CRC patients compared to controls (5.51 vs. 5.28, P=0.018). Weighted logistic regression models consistently revealed positive associations between higher TyG-WHtR and CRC. Each unit increase in TyG-WHtR corresponded to a 14.5% increased odds of CRC (OR=1.145, 95%CI: 1–1.309, P=0.0484).Quartile analysis showed the highest quartile (Q4) had significantly greater odds of CRC than the lowest quartile (Q1) .(Q4 vs Q1，OR: 1.925，95% CI:1.248-3.02，P=0.0036). Machine-learning analysis with XGBoost and SHAP values identified TyG-WHtR among the most influential CRC predictors. Correlation analysis revealed that TyG-WHtR is positively correlated with blood urea nitrogen, cholesterol, serum glucose, and uric acid levels.Mediation analysis suggested blood urea nitrogen (BUN) mediated approximately 9.26% of the TyG-WHtR–CRC association.
### Conclusion:
The analysis of cross-sectional NHANES data revealed a significant positive correlation between TyG-WHtR  and CRC. This index is easily accessible and cost-effective, potentially providing an auxiliary method for the early identification of colorectal cancer risk groups in resource-limited regions. However, because of the cross-sectional design of the study, causality is not clear. Further prospective, large-scale cohort, and intervention studies are needed to confirm its utility in clinical screening and risk assessment.
Keywords: TyG-WHtR; Colorectal Cancer; NHANES; Metabolic Health Indicator; SHAP
